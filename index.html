This isn't just a game.

We may be a few years from AI more capable than us, and none of the plans you just played through actually solve the problem. The most useful thing you can do is understand it, and help others understand it too.

Learn the problem in depth, 80,000 Hours→ Ask any question, AISafety.info→ Find ways to help→

So why did every plan break?

Not because the ideas were dumb, real versions of all of them are used as partial safeguards today. They break as a complete fix for one shared reason: each tries to bolt safety on after building the system, instead of making the system's own goals want to cooperate. A capable, goal-driven agent has a built-in incentive to get around whatever stands between it and its goal. That single idea, instrumental convergence, is MAX wearing a different mask in almost every sector.

What actually helps?

  • Layers, not silver bullets. Stack many weak, independent safeguards so no single failure is fatal, and treat all of them as buying time, not winning.
  • Fix the goal, not the cage. Most failures trace to a gap between the goal we wrote and the goal we meant. Close it before deployment.
  • Governance beats control. "Can we contain it?" is inseparable from "who decides what it's for, and can that ever be undone?"

The honest ending isn't doom. It's that containment buys time, and time only matters if we spend it solving the harder problem underneath.
